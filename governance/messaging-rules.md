# Messaging rules: consent, opt-out and quiet hours

These are the rules the software enforces when it sends a patient a message. They are written to be read by a clinician, an information governance lead and a developer. They are not legal advice: the controller confirms the legal basis in its own DPIA ([`dpia.md`](dpia.md)).

## What kind of message this is

Everything this system sends is a **service message**: an appointment reminder, a change to an appointment, or an answer to something the patient sent. It is sent to deliver care the patient has been offered, not to promote anything.

- Under **PECR**, the consent rule for electronic *marketing* does not apply to a service message.
- Under **UK GDPR** the controller still needs a lawful basis (usually public task for an NHS body) and the patient's wish to stop is honoured regardless.
- **There is no marketing category in this system.** If one is ever added it must require an active `marketing` consent and a PECR-compliant opt-in before it can send, and that change needs its own review.

## The rules, and where they are enforced

| Rule | Enforced in | Tested by |
|---|---|---|
| A patient who texts **STOP** (or **STOPIO**) gets no more SMS, WhatsApp or Apple Messages from us, at that number, until they text **START** (**DECHRAU**). | `communication::consent`, `inbound` | `pilot7_consent_test.rs` |
| A STOP from a number we cannot match, or that two patients share, **still stops** that number. | `inbound` (opt-out held against the address as well as the person) | `texting_stop_stops_that_number_even_when_we_cannot_tell_who_it_is` |
| STOP does not silence email, and an email opt-out does not silence text. | `communication::consent::is_opted_out` | `a_patient_who_opted_out_of_the_text_channels_is_sent_the_email_only_and_then_nothing` |
| **No send path can skip an opt-out**, including a letter generated by hand. | `CommunicationService::generate_letter` (the one place every send passes) | `nothing_can_bypass_an_opt_out_not_even_a_direct_letter` |
| Staff can record or lift an opt-out the patient gave by phone or in writing. | `/api/patients/{id}/channel-optouts` | `staff_can_record_list_and_lift_an_opt_out_over_the_api` |
| **Quiet hours** (default 21:00 to 08:00 clinic time, `PAS_QUIET_HOURS`, `off` to disable) hold back SMS, WhatsApp and Apple Messages. A reminder due then **waits for the morning**; it is not dropped and does not switch to another channel overnight. | `communication::reminders` | `quiet_hours_defer_the_reminder_without_losing_it`; unit tests for the window |
| Email and the secure portal are not held back by quiet hours: they are read when the patient chooses. | `communication::consent::is_intrusive` | unit test |

## The reminder ladder

Reminders climb a ladder (default 7 days, 4 days, 3 days, 24 hours before). **Every patient who has not confirmed, asked to reschedule or used a link is escalated**: a second touch on another channel, then a phone call by a person. The test is a patient's action, never an email "open", which proves nothing. Every step is logged per appointment. Links in messages are single-use, carry no personal data, and **opening one never acts**: only a deliberate tap does, and Cancel asks twice. The caps, quiet hours and opt-outs above apply to every step.

## What this does not cover yet

- **Delivery receipts and bounces** (NOT-4) and **real carriers** (NOT-3) are not built, so nothing is sent to a real person. These rules are in place before that, as intended.
- Quiet hours are applied by the automatic reminder scan. A message a member of staff sends by hand is not held back (a person chose to send it), but it still respects an opt-out.
- The **portal** and **print** are not opt-out channels here; a patient who wants no letters or portal messages needs the controller's own process.
- A patient cannot yet opt out through the portal (the `portal` source is reserved in the data model). Staff record it.
- Opt-outs are never deleted automatically; the retention schedule ([`retention-schedule.md`](retention-schedule.md)) must say how long they are kept. Keep them at least as long as the contact details they protect.
